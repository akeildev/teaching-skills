# Mode catalog

Modes are teaching functions. Read each description as a decision aid, in light of the learner and the unit. They overlap on purpose: a scene can raise a question, a worked example can pause for a prediction. List each function a section really performs.

Rules for every mode:

- Any mode can repeat or go unused. If none fits a needed teaching action, name and define a new one, and leave a note suggesting it.
- Examples, settings, order, length, and creative treatment are yours to choose. The user's brief outranks stylistic habits, including any implied by these descriptions.
- Everything stays faithful to the source and keeps its qualifications. "Produces" means what the finished lesson must contain. The plan names the mode and its job; whoever builds the lesson writes it.

## Orient and motivate

**Hook.** Gives the learner a reason to care before they have one. They meet a surprise, consequence, or unresolved situation tied to what comes next. Use it when relevance is not yet felt. Produces the situation and its link to the coming idea. A hook can pay off quickly; Big question stays open across several ideas.

**Big question.** Organizes several ideas as one inquiry the learner cannot yet answer. They hold the question and watch evidence accumulate toward it. Use it when units gain meaning from a shared problem. Produces the question and how later material resolves it. Interactive question wants an answer now; this one wants an answer by the end.

**Objectives.** Shows the destination when scope or the standard of success is unclear, usually at a course or lesson boundary. The learner sees what they will be able to explain, do, or judge, and why that is useful. Produces those statements at the specificity the task needs. A sentence may be enough, and established objectives carry forward until the goal changes.

## Explain

**Definition.** Fixes the meaning of a term that blocks understanding or communication. The learner gets the meaning plus enough examples or non-examples to see its edges. Produces the meaning and its boundary. Concept explains why something holds; Comparison handles confusion between two things.

**Concept.** Builds understanding of a principle or relationship when recognizing its name is not enough. The learner follows the central idea, why it holds here, and its limits. Produces the explanation with reasoning or examples aimed at the actual difficulty. Prefer a more specific mode when comparing cases, inspecting structure, or solving a problem is the real action.

**Model/diagram.** Makes structure, dependencies, flows, or hidden states inspectable when they are hard to hold in mind. The learner reads a representation and uses it to explain or predict. Produces the representation, how its parts map to the subject, and its simplifications. It may be revealed step by step; when an unfolding situation carries the explanation, use Animated scene.

**Process.** Explains how an operation runs when order, state changes, branches, or repetition determine what happens. The learner follows starting conditions, transitions, and completion conditions, and can predict the next step. Produces the steps with reasons for the consequential ones. Worked example shows one instance in full; Timeline fits when dates or duration are the insight.

**Timeline.** Shows development when timing, duration, overlap, or historical order matters. The learner sees events on a meaningful scale tied to evidence. Produces the events and what changed between them, keeping sequence distinct from demonstrated cause. Process fits repeatable logic; Animated scene fits when being in the moment teaches more than inspecting chronology.

**Comparison.** Makes a consequential distinction usable when learners confuse alternatives or must choose between them. They examine cases aligned on the dimensions that matter and work out what the differences imply. Produces the cases, the dimensions, and the implication. It has worked when the learner can distinguish or select in a new case; a feature list alone falls short.

**Analogy.** Bridges from a relationship the learner already knows to one they do not. They map the parts, find where the resemblance stops, and return to the real subject. Produces the mapping and its limit. Judge it by whether it supports correct inferences; explain directly when the analogy is as hard as the idea or points the wrong way.

## Show it in use

**Worked example.** Demonstrates knowledge guiding a concrete solution, interpretation, or decision. The learner watches a method applied, with the choices and reasons visible. Produces the full instance, step by step, with transferable reasoning. Practice hands the work to the learner; Case study fits when contextual tradeoffs are the point.

**Case study.** Develops judgment in a realistic situation where evidence, constraints, or competing goals interact. The learner examines plausible responses and their tradeoffs. Produces the situation, the interpretations, and what each costs, real or clearly labeled as authored. Worked example fits when a method is the main point; add Practice when the learner must make the decision themselves.

## Have the learner do it

**Interactive question.** Elicits a focused response when retrieval, prediction, or explanation can expose understanding or challenge a misconception. The learner commits to an answer before the explanation. Produces the prompt, a sound answer, and feedback for likely errors. The commitment before a reveal is what defines it: a labeling or sorting task after the explanation is Practice, as is any sustained task or artifact.

**Practice.** Builds or tests the ability to apply knowledge. The learner performs, constructs, diagnoses, or decides, with support matched to readiness. Produces the task, the available information, success criteria, and feedback, and says whether it is supported practice or an independent check. Worked example comes first when they have yet to see the method.

**Reflection.** Improves judgment by examining something specific the learner did, saw, or changed their mind about. They explain what happened, why, what supports that, or what they would change. Produces a prompt anchored in something they can actually examine. Interactive question checks knowledge; Teach-it organizes an explanation for someone else.

**Teach-it.** Exposes gaps by having the learner explain an idea to a named audience. They produce the reasoning, examples, and limits that audience needs. Produces the audience, the explanation task, and feedback criteria for accuracy and completeness. Fluent explanation is weak evidence of a practical capability, so pair it with performance when the target is doing something.

## Consolidate

**Summary.** Reconnects substantial material before moving on. The learner sees relationships, conclusions, and limits already taught, at whatever scope helps. Produces the synthesis, which can span units. It presents rather than checks recall. Memory anchor fits one cue; Study sheet fits lookup.

**Memory anchor.** Supports later recall of something already understood through a compact phrase, image, mnemonic, or rule. The learner gets a cue they can reconstruct. Produces the cue, what it retrieves, and any qualification that prevents misuse. Use Concept for first understanding.

**Study sheet.** Supports repeated lookup during practice or review. The learner gets a compact reference arranged for finding things. Produces the rules, cues, examples, or cautions the task needs, and says when using it is allowed, so assisted performance is not mistaken for recall. Summary is an in-lesson synthesis.

## Enact

**Animated scene.** A vivid conceptual situation unfolds through meaningful actions, interactions, changes, or reveals. It may show why an idea matters, establish historical context, demonstrate a mechanism or consequence, or make a distinction memorable enough to replay mentally. Use it when watching something happen teaches more than being told. Characters and plot are optional; narration and clarifying labels are allowed. The scene's action carries the idea, so moving text or chart cards alone belong under Timeline or Model/diagram. Produces a sequence of moments whose action carries the teaching point, with narration or captions. Label invented illustrations and metaphor limits. Historical details come from the source.
