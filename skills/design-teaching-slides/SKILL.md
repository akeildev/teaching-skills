# Design teaching slides

Turn a teaching plan into a finished deck in its real destination, with every slide checked as rendered.

```
- [ ] 1. Settle the style
- [ ] 2. Read the inputs
- [ ] 3. Design the pictures
- [ ] 4. Build the whole deck
- [ ] 5. Check every slide and deliver
```

## 1. Settle the style

Use one of the user's existing styles; [the style reference](references/styles.md) says where they are. A style named in the request is the answer. Otherwise show two or three with screenshots, recommend one, and wait. For a style made in another session, find its slides first, and ask only if none exist or versions conflict. The style's own slides outrank any design tool's default style advice.

## 2. Read the inputs

For a plan from `plan-teaching-modes`, read [the input contract](references/input-contract.md). The plan gives the main point, each unit's modes, their jobs and the order; the outline gives what to teach and the evidence. Every slide serves the main point. You write the lesson: wording, examples, questions with answers and feedback, pictures and scenes. Keep the plan's modes and order, and add no claims beyond the evidence. Flag a gap that matters and carry on with the rest.

Slides teach on their own unless the brief includes narration. In Paper, also read [the Paper notes](references/paper-production.md).

## 3. Design the pictures

Screenshot varied slides of the chosen style to learn its look: how it draws shapes, people, colour, type and texture. That is all they are for: their people, objects and scenes belong to other lessons.

Then design each picture from first principles:

1. Name what the learner must be able to say after the slide, and the relationship behind it: what does what, what depends on what, what can change while the rest stays.
2. Choose whatever representation shows that relationship most clearly, at whatever level of realism or abstraction works best. It needn't be real; how its parts are placed and connected should make the relationship obvious at a glance. Include only what the relationship needs.
3. Draw it in the style's look, labelled with the lesson's real names, so the picture's logic is the concept's logic. If the point is that a bridge stands because of its shape, show the shape carrying the load, not just a bridge.

Test it: someone who sees only the picture should guess the relationship correctly.

## 4. Build the whole deck

Build every slide of the plan, in its order: each item's modes, with nothing skipped or reordered. Once the first slides pass the step 5 checks, record what they settled (recurring people and objects, what colours mean in this course) in the deck's tracker, so later slides match. Expand that kit whenever a slide needs another object, person or setting to show its relationship better, and record the addition too. For a large, resumed or multi-style deck, follow [batch production](references/batch-production.md). If the medium can't deliver something the plan needs, such as an animation, tell the user and settle it with them.

## 5. Check every slide and deliver

Screenshot each slide and check:

- It is clearly the chosen style, and the picture leads and shows the point the learner should remember.
- The picture's layout shows the concept's relationship, so its logic matches the concept's.
- Each question makes the learner think about the main point or the unit's key idea, not a side detail.
- Text is short, sits beside what it names, and is readable at viewing size. A point that needs a paragraph gets another slide.
- Questions appear unanswered first; no colour, tick or fading gives the answer away before the reveal.
- Worked examples show correct intermediate steps.
- Diagrams keep direction, grouping and quantities; an invented chart is labelled illustrative.
- Quotation marks are only for exact source text; a person whose pronouns the source doesn't give is "they" or named.
- Production notes stay in the tracker, off the slides.

Then check the deck against the plan: every mode of every item has its slide or slides, in the plan's order, and the tracker maps each slide to its item and mode. Fix, re-check the changed slide, and check the flow across slides. Deliver the deck link, what was rendered and checked, and anything unresolved; say what is built versus tested with a learner.
