# Use the teaching plan

Two upstream skills feed this one:

- `organize-teachable-concepts`: the outline. Units with targets and success checks, their components (the checklist), supporting material, exercises and review items, each tied to source evidence. Its `outline.md` is the readable view.
- `plan-teaching-modes`: the plan. Lessons in order, each holding units (and course-level items such as an opener) with rough minutes; for each, the modes and the one-line job of each; units left out and why; questions for the user. Its `view.md` is the readable view.

Build from both. The plan says what each part of the lesson must do; the outline supplies what to teach and the evidence for it. You write the lesson: wording, examples, questions with answers and feedback, pictures and scenes. Keep each claim's caveats from the outline, and carry its review items (uncertain names, opinions, time-sensitive claims) onto the slides as the outline marks them.

If only an outline exists, route to `plan-teaching-modes` before designing. A plain readable brief that already settles the modes works too.

Keep a mapping from slides to lesson and unit IDs, so omissions and corrections can be traced.
